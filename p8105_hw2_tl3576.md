``` r
baseline_df =
  read_csv(
    "data/MCI_baseline.csv",
    skip = 1,
    na = "."
  ) |> 
  janitor::clean_names() |> 
  mutate(
    sex = case_match(
      sex,
      1 ~ "male",
      0 ~ "female"
    ),
    apoe4 = case_match(
      apoe4,
      1 ~ "carrier",
      0 ~ "non-carrier"

  rename(study_id = id) |> 
  filter(age_at_onset > current_age | is.na(age_at_onset))
```

When importing the baseline dataset, I skipped the first row and treated
periods as missing values. Then, I recoded sex and APOE4 status to the
appropriate labels. To meet the inclusion criteria of no MCI at
baseline, I excluded participants whose age at onset was at or before
their age at baseline, retaining those who developed MCI after baseline
or remained MCI-free during follow-up.

After cleaning the dataset, there were 479 participants. Of these, 93
(19.42%) developed MCI. On average, the baseline age was 65 years. Among
women, the proportion of those who were APOE4 carriers was 0.3.

When importing the amyloid dataset, I skipped the first row and treated
Na and NA as missing values. I used the `pivot_longer()` function to
reshape the data from wide to long format because visits were spread
across multiple columns. Then, I recoded baseline to 0 and converted
year to a numeric variable.

``` r
mci_df =
  left_join(baseline_df, amyloid_df, by = "study_id")
```

There were 479 participants who appeared in both the baseline and
amyloid datasets. Key variables in the MCI dataset include `study_id`,
`age_at_onset`, `year`, and `amyloid_ratio`. Among participants who
developed MCI, the mean age at onset was 70 years. The average amyloid
ratio was 0.111 at baseline, 0.11 at year 2, 0.11 at year 4, 0.109 at
year 6, and 0.108 at year 8.
